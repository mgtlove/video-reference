# Ken Burns and PBS audio, measured (g5WkOr565I4)

Silent graph, 2x, audio-thread sampling over two windows: the open (0:00 to 12:00, 4227 samples) and the Lexington passage (90:00 to 95:00, 1802 samples). The mix is a broadcast mix: narration, readings, music and effects on one track, so these numbers describe the whole soundtrack, not the voice alone.

- Loudness: median RMS 0.103, 90th percentile 0.154, 99th 0.186; 10th percentile 0.035. Dynamic range 14.5 dB on this scale, the smallest of the set, which is the broadcast mix talking: music fills the gaps the voice leaves, so the floor never drops. The histogram is a single hump from 0.04 to 0.16 peaking at 0.12.
- Silence: one true silence, 10.8 s at the start (black and the PBS card). The 68 s "silence" the captions show at 3:24 to 4:32 is not silent: under the titles the track sits at a median of 0.066 and never below 0.014. That is music with no words. The soundtrack is continuous; the words are not.
- Pauses in the words: measured against the music the pauses do not register as low RMS, so the caption gaps are the better measure here (34 gaps over 2 s in the episode, 136 s in total, from the first pass). Within the voice the 0.02 threshold finds 32 dips of 0.3 s or more in 17 minutes, five over a second, the longest 1.4 s at 9:52 (between a reading and the Native reading) and 1.2 s at 2:33 (before "In the spring of 1754").
- Loudest cues: the two explosions (4:57 at 0.211, 0:56 at 0.174) and the gunfire at 9:45 (0.171), then the thesis sentence at 5:01 "The American Revolution was not just a clash between Englishmen over Indian land" (0.161, 0.154) and Abigail Adams' reading at 6:50 (0.156). The effects are the loudest things in the film; the narrator's loudest line is the thesis.
- Quietest cues: 89:58 "To fight with whom?" (0.002, a reading that starts from near silence), the drums at 91:46 (0.016), the music cue at 10:11 (0.041), the gulls at 90:16 (0.052). The quietest moments are the starts of readings and the sound cues that introduce a scene.
- Timbre: spectral centroid median 2821 Hz (1940 to 3781), the brightest and widest of the set because music and effects are in the measure; the 10th-to-90th range of 1841 Hz is twice any voice-only video's. This is the measurement saying "orchestrated."
- The two windows differ: the open (median 0.100, 10th percentile 0.033) is quieter and more varied than the Lexington passage (median 0.106, 10th percentile 0.025, 99th 0.177), which is narration over drums and night sounds with fewer readings.
- Pattern: words sit in a continuous bed; effects are louder than any word; the thesis is the loudest sentence; readings begin from near silence and attributions end them. Pace is in the gaps between words, not in the loudness of the track.
