Three delivery families fall out of the numbers. The cut voice (Fireship, Casually Explained, How Money Works): no pause over a second, a dynamic range under 19 dB, a narrow centroid, words at a normal rate with the gaps removed, which is what makes 200 to 220 wpm sound unhurried. The teaching voice (Maarek, AWS, IBM): pauses every six to ten seconds that are real (a breath, a wait, a word being written), a range of 30 to 64 dB, a brighter and wider timbre, questions louder than answers. The narrator (Ken Burns): slower than all of them with silence as structure.

## Word and picture: the relation each one lives on

- **Fireship**: two parallel channels. The voice argues; the picture editorialises. Half the visual jokes are jokes the voice never makes (the gangster meme, the whisper caption, the twins' labels). Evidence is a screenshot shown whole, then pushed into the highlighted line the voice is quoting.
- **Casually Explained**: one channel, confirmed a beat late. The frame changes on the last word 61 times in 78; the picture is the punchline's receipt. Real photographs are the straight man; the joke is what is drawn or written on them.
- **How Money Works**: literal nouns. Every sentence has a picture that is literal to one noun in it (ship, facade, flag, soldiers), from a small vocabulary that repeats until it is invisible; the channel's own devices (clipping, badge-man, checklist, table) mark structure. The one time the picture reads with the voice is the highlighter on a cited document.
- **Stephane Maarek**: the picture is the sentence's outline. A bullet appears as its sentence starts; an arrow is drawn as its sentence is spoken; nothing is ever ahead of the voice; the exam framing and the deferrals exist only in the voice.
- **AWS Developers**: announce, do, explain. The voice says what it will click, the cursor clicks it, the voice says why. Diagrams restate what was just done or what is about to be done, in the sentence's own nouns.
- **IBM Technology**: the picture is the sentence's skeleton with the verbs removed. Lists are written one item per spoken item; every board resolves into one arrow chain; the silence is where the word appears.
- **Ken Burns and PBS**: the picture is the artefact and the camera is the reading. The move reveals the headline on the last word; the camera travels down a list as the voice reads it; a route draws as the sentence runs. Landscapes carry the catalogue sentences and the readings; sound cues are literal (sawdust, forge, surf) where the picture is atmospheric; witnesses say what the narrator cannot.
