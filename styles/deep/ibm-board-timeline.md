# IBM Technology lightboard: the board's life, every 10 seconds (bs1qPy_CWkM, 7:14)

Method: frames at every 10 s (44 frames) viewed as montage sheets with the caption cues under each; camera cuts from frame differencing at a 0.045 threshold on a dark frame (84 events, median 3 s, many are hand movements; the real framing changes are the ones noted below). Raw observation.

## Four boards, not one

## Patterns in how the board is used

- One heading word per board, written large and first, in the colour that is not the list colour. Lists are written one item per spoken item, so the viewer never reads ahead of the voice.
- The thesis phrase ("prompt -> production") is written twice, on board 1 as "PLAN > code" and on board 4 as the title line, and the first line of the video ("moving from prompt to production") is the last thing written.
- Arrows carry the argument: idea -> outcome (crossed out as the naive view), prompt -> code, list -> repos/services/pipelines, Output + evidence -> Confidence. Every board resolves into one arrow chain.
- Crossing out is used once (idea -> outcome) and the crossed-out item stays on the board for the rest of the section as the thing being argued against.
- The circle (board 2) is the only enclosure drawn after the fact; it is drawn while she says "all into one workflow", so the gesture is the meaning.
- The wireframe (board 1) is the only picture; everything else is words and arrows. The wireframe's crossed-out boxes are not explained in words.
- Text height on the board is about 3 to 4 percent of frame height for list items, 6 percent for the headings.
- The marker is held throughout, even when not writing; she points with it. Hands are in frame in most frames.
- Nothing is pre-drawn except the house outline and, on board 2, the heading; everything else appears on camera. Writing time is talk time; she keeps speaking while writing, and the 2 to 5 s silences in the transcript fall on the longer words.

## Camera

Three framings: wide (board visible both sides of her, waist up), medium (shoulders up, board text partly out of frame), and a tighter medium when she is speaking without writing (4:10 to 4:30, 6:20 to 6:30). Cuts happen on sentence boundaries and never mid-word. The presenter stays centred; the board content lives in the left and right thirds. The lower third (name, title) is on screen for the first 10 s only; the IBM mark is small, bottom right, throughout.

## Legibility note

The handwriting is mirrored in post so it reads correctly, which means she is writing backwards from the viewer's side or the whole image is flipped; the parting of her hair and the hand she writes with would tell which. Strokes glow because the marker is fluorescent under edge lighting; the glow is the lightboard, not an effect.
