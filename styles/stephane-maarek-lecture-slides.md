## In two sentences

A certification course lecture: one slide deck with progressive builds and a labelled diagram, narrated by a teacher who defines each term as he names it and says what the exam will ask. Nothing on the frame moves except the next bullet appearing.

## Voice

A teacher at the front of a room, second person and first person plural ("what we'll do is"), even and unhurried. Defines every term in the same breath as it is named ("route 53 is a managed DNS and DNS stands for domain name system"), tells the learner what matters ("the most common that will be asked of you"), promises depth later so the overview stays short, and is candid about cost ("just so you know"). No jokes, no anecdotes.

## Language

Discourse markers do the work of punctuation: "so" 18 times in 660 words, "now" 6, "well" 5, "okay" 2; "you" 15, "we" 9. Opens on a marker ("okay so let's talk about route 53") and closes with a handoff to the next lecture. The slide is the script's outline: he reads the bullet in slightly expanded words, then adds the reason.

## Visuals

A light grey slide with a white content area, a thin light-grey heading, black bullets and sub-bullets, the service icon top right in the product's orange, and an orange wedge across the bottom carrying the copyright tag. Bullets appear one at a time as the narration reaches them (appear, no motion). The diagram slide (1:00 to 1:55) carries the whole lesson: a laptop icon on the left, the service icon top right, a document icon bottom right, and dotted arrows added one by one, each labelled along its length with the request it represents. Colour encodes cases (blue for the public example, orange for the private one). Text is small for video: sub-bullets about 2.5 percent of frame height, lines up to 70 characters. No presenter, no cursor, no pointer; the build is the pointer.
