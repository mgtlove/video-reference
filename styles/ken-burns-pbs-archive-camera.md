# Style: Ken Burns and PBS (archive camera)

- Video studied: "The American Revolution | Full Episode 1 | In Order to Be Free (May 1754 to May 1775)" (g5WkOr565I4), 1:53:39, Ken Burns and PBS, 16K likes
- URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g5WkOr565I4
- Studied on: 30 Sep 2026, by the video lane chat, with the built-in browser, muted; broadcast captions fetched for the whole episode and read closely for the first ten minutes; eleven frames viewed, including a pair five seconds apart to measure the camera move
- Credits shown: PBS bug throughout; narrator, readers and interviewees are credited in the film's titles
- What is kept here: words and numbers only. No frame, clip, logo, thumbnail or caption text is copied into this repo.

## In two sentences

A broadcast documentary built from period artefacts under a slowly moving camera, present-day landscapes at low light, interviewees in dark rooms, and three registers of speech (narrator, actors reading documents, witnesses). It is the origin of the "Ken Burns effect" and the calmest register in this set.

## Voice

The narrator is measured, past tense, declarative, never addressing the viewer, never hedging, never signposting; the structure is felt through music and silence. Period voices are read by actors and attributed after the quote ("Thomas Paine" at 0:55, after 33 s of reading), so the words land before the name. Interviewees speak in the present tense and first person and keep their hesitations.

## Language

Long sentences built from lists in threes and place names (average 17.2 words, 104 over 30 in 812), read aloud slowly. The open is a quotation, an explosion, a slow drum, a second quotation, and the narrator's first sentence at 1:33; the story proper starts at 2:23 and the thesis sentence arrives at 5:01. Every section runs narrator, document voice, witness, sound cue. Speaker blocks across the episode: narrator 75, document voices 65, interviewees 23; 27 music cues, 93 sound cues.

## Visuals

Four kinds of frame: a period artefact (engraving, newspaper page, painting, map, letter) under a slow camera; a present-day landscape at golden hour or dusk (light rays in a forest, silhouetted hills, an interior lit through the roof); an interviewee in a dark room; a hand-drawn period map with animated troop blocks and arrows in two colours (the only diagram). The camera move on artefacts was measured on a newspaper page at 2:36 to 2:41: a slow tilt down with a slight pull back, about 3 percent of frame height per second, timed so the headline is revealed with the last word of the sentence. Holds of 5 to 12 s per artefact; paintings cropped into a detail and eased out, or held full frame. Colour is the artefact's own (cream paper, black ink, oils) and the landscapes are warm and low-key. No text on the frame outside titles and lower thirds; every name is spoken. Sound design carries the transitions.
