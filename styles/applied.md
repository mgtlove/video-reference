# Applied: what each studied style suggests for our own videos

This is the filtered layer, kept out of the study notes on purpose. The notes in `styles/*.md` and `styles/deep/` record what each creator does; this file is where the craft rules and the house rules are applied to that record. Every claim here cites a rule id from `craft/` or names the guardrail; the look-pack numbers live in `looks.json`. Moved out of the study notes on 1 Oct 2026.

## IBM Technology (lightboard)

Source note: `styles/ibm-technology-lightboard.md`; deep pass: `styles/deep/ibm-board-timeline.md`, `styles/deep/ibm-rhetoric.md`, `styles/deep/ibm-audio.md`

Transfers: the black ground with glowing marker strokes drawing on with the voice (the kit's seeded strokes and `createDrawable`, see `studies/anime-js`); structure drawn as a container first and filled second (house, circle, list); one thesis line at the open and close; silence while a drawing completes (C-PACE-5, C-PACE-7 within the 8 s still ceiling because the stroke is moving); three markers at most (C-COL-4, C-COMP-13). This is the closest look to the existing teaching layer, and the one the roadmap names first. The stakeholders' formal tone is this look with one marker and no glow.

Does not transfer: the presenter (we have no one on camera and will not fake one), the mirrored-glass trick, the IBM set and marks. Handwriting text at 3 to 4 percent of height is under our 54 px floor (5 percent) for anything the narration depends on (C-TYPE-3), so our marker text is larger and shorter.

## Fireship (The Code Report)

Source note: `styles/fireship-code-report.md`; deep pass: `styles/deep/fireship-shotlog.md`, `styles/deep/fireship-rhetoric.md`, `styles/deep/fireship-audio.md`

Transfers: the fixed format with signature lines at fixed places (our video brief can hold a house structure the same way); evidence as screenshots with ids, slightly rotated with a shadow so they read as exhibits, one accent per graphic (C-COL-4); the three-band diagram as a way to show levels; a demo on a real repository with an honest verdict and numbers; large labels (6 percent clears C-TYPE-3). The cut rate (3.3 s) is at our ceiling (C-CAM-8 uses 4 s as a ceiling, not a target) and would be halved for a formal audience.

Does not transfer: the jokes, the memes and animal clips, the rotated screenshots of other people's posts (we screenshot only our own product and evidence), the 220 wpm (our range is 140 to 170, C-PACE-1), the monospace prompt text at 3 percent (under the 54 px floor).

## AWS Developers (studio walkthrough)

Source note: `styles/aws-developers-studio-walkthrough.md`; deep pass: `styles/deep/aws-shotlog.md`, `styles/deep/aws-rhetoric.md`, `styles/deep/aws-audio.md`

Transfers: the questions-then-outline open and the on-screen checklist that the video then ticks off (our brief's scene list can become that checklist); announce, do, explain per step; the branded frame around the working screen, which is what our rail-and-stage layout already is; two brand accents and nothing else (C-COL-4); 164 wpm sits inside our range (C-PACE-1). This is the "walkthrough" scene kind's natural register.

Does not transfer: the presenter and the set; picture-in-picture (nothing to put in it); a real screen recording scaled to fit, which puts product text far under the 54 px floor (C-TYPE-3, C-COMP-16): our recreated screens show the region that matters at native size and push in (C-CAM-3), which is why we recreate rather than record. Also the length: 44 minutes against our 5 to 6 minute ceiling (C-PACE-9, C-PACE-10), so a walkthrough is a series of short parts.

## How Money Works (clippings)

Source note: `styles/how-money-works-clippings.md`; deep pass: `styles/deep/hmw-shotlog.md`, `styles/deep/hmw-rhetoric.md`, `styles/deep/hmw-audio.md`

Transfers: the evidence-first frame, where a cited document is the picture and a highlighter moves along the line being read (our capture folder's evidence with ids, shown as the clipping with its id in the corner); the slow drift on every still so a document never reads as a slide (C-CAM-4, C-CAM-5: 5 to 10 percent scale over the hold); sections that open with a plain question; sources named in the narration; the promise-then-deliver structure. Clippings are the natural look for a "why this matters" opener over the release notes, a policy or a customer quote.

Does not transfer: licensed footage and broadcast clips (we own nothing like it and will not scrape it), the sarcasm, 198 wpm (C-PACE-1), sentences over 30 words (C-PACE-2), text carried inside a clipping at whatever size it happens to be (C-TYPE-3: our clippings are recreated at a readable size).

## Casually Explained (doodle)

Source note: `styles/casually-explained-doodle.md`; deep pass: `styles/deep/casually-shotlog.md`, `styles/deep/casually-rhetoric.md`, `styles/deep/casually-audio.md`

Transfers: the white ground with a single black line weight and one accent, the cheapest look to render and the most legible (C-COL-1 at 21:1); held frames released on the last word, which is exactly a beat per sentence (C-PACE-4); a drawn figure over a real screenshot, so a recreated screen gets a human reaction without a presenter; sparse hand-lettered labels; a pop or slide of 200 ms for each added element; no music bed, so narration is the only sound. This is the roadmap's "cartoon doodle" look and the one that fits a "concept opener" for a formal audience once the jokes are removed, because the frame is so quiet.

Does not transfer: the comedy (the whole point of the channel, and not ours to imitate), 216 wpm (C-PACE-1), stock charts and AI-generated paintings as gags, hand lettering under the 54 px floor (C-TYPE-3).

## Stephane Maarek (lecture slides)

Source note: `styles/stephane-maarek-lecture-slides.md`; deep pass: `styles/deep/maarek-shotlog.md`, `styles/deep/maarek-rhetoric.md`, `styles/deep/maarek-audio.md`

Transfers: the progressive build, where the slide grows only when the narration reaches the point (C-PACE-4, C-COMP-6 reading order); the labelled-arrow diagram that becomes the whole lesson, with the request text written along the arrow (our diagram scene kind, `studies/anime-js` motion path for the arrow draw); colour that encodes cases rather than decorates (C-COL-5); the product's own icons; a term defined in the breath it is named (our narration rule); a close that hands off to the next part. This is the "lecture" register, the safest for a formal audience, and the one a formal audience will recognise.

Does not transfer: text sizes (2.5 to 3.5 percent fails C-TYPE-1 to 3) and 70-character lines (C-TYPE-4 is 42); the flat slide frame with a wedge, which is the creator's brand; 203 wpm (C-PACE-1); a whole lesson on one slide with nothing else to look at, which our still ceiling (C-PACE-7) forbids beyond 8 to 12 s without a build.

## Ken Burns and PBS (archive camera)

Source note: `styles/ken-burns-pbs-archive-camera.md`; deep pass: `styles/deep/kenburns-shotlog.md`, `styles/deep/kenburns-rhetoric.md`, `styles/deep/kenburns-audio.md`

Transfers: the camera grammar over a still, measured here at 2 to 4 percent per second with the reveal landing on the last word (C-CAM-4, C-CAM-5, the named Ken Burns primitive in the kit), applied to our recreated screens and evidence documents in place of engravings; attribution after the quote for customer or stakeholder quotations; three registers of voice (narrator, quoted document, witness) as a structure for a case-study video; silence and sound cues as punctuation, which our formal tone can afford (C-PACE-5, C-PACE-15); no text on the frame while the camera moves (C-COMP-7); low, warm light for any landscape or office still. This is the "calm and informative" register Matthew asked for, and the only one of the seven that runs under 130 wpm.

Does not transfer: 123 wpm is under our floor (C-PACE-1, 130 wpm) and would be raised to 140 for a five-minute video; holds of 12 s only work with a moving camera (C-PACE-7); the artefacts, footage, music and interviews are the film's; the length.
