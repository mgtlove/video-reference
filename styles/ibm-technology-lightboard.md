# Style: IBM Technology (lightboard explainer)

- Video studied: "Prompt to Production: The Future of AI Code Workflows" (bs1qPy_CWkM), 7:14, IBM Technology, 47.6K views at 2 days
- URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bs1qPy_CWkM
- Studied on: 30 Sep 2026, by the video lane chat, with the built-in browser, muted; manual captions fetched; frames viewed at about ten points across the run
- Credits shown: presenter Sam Anthony, Data Scientist, IBM (lower third at 0:05); "think" series ident; IBM logo bottom right
- What is kept here: words and numbers only. No frame, clip, logo, thumbnail or caption text is copied into this repo.

## In two sentences

A presenter stands behind a glass lightboard on a black set and draws the explanation in neon marker while talking to camera, so the drawing and the speech are one performance. The channel's explainers all share this set, so the look itself is the brand.

## Voice

Second person, present tense, a colleague explaining at a whiteboard. Plain declaratives, short sentences (average 13.8 words, one over 30 in the whole video). No jokes, no anecdotes beyond "I can review that myself", no hype. One thesis line repeated at the open and the close ("prompt to production").

## Language

Rhetorical questions in a run ("What changed? Why did it change? What could break?") that then become the drawn list. A building analogy (concrete before design) carried through the whole piece. Terms are named and drawn at the same moment, so the noun on the board is the definition. Chapters about every 40 s, listed in the description. Silences of 2 to 5 s are left in while she draws (six of them), which is unusual on YouTube and reads as calm.

## Visuals

Black ground; the presenter in a black shirt, centred; handwriting in three or four neon markers (violet, cyan, pink, mint) with a glow around each stroke, appearing live as drawn and mirrored so it reads correctly. The drawings are structural: a house outline holding a bullet list, a wireframe of a web page with crossed-out boxes, a big circle with an arrow list, single words drawn large ("Output", "coordination"). Board text is about 3 to 4 percent of frame height per line, on the left and right of the presenter. Three camera framings (wide, medium, and pushes between) with cuts every 10 to 20 s; hands gesture in frame. Lower third at the open only; no B-roll, no screen recordings, no memes.
