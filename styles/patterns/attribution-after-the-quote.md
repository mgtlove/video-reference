# Attribution after the quote

Group: argument. Say it like: "quote first, name second", "who said that", "read the quote then credit it", "customer quote".

## Observed

- Ken Burns and PBS (g5WkOr565I4): every reading ends with the name ("Thomas Paine," "Shingas, Lenape Nation," "[Washington]"); the words land, then the attribution. The frame under the reading is the artefact or a landscape; the witness's name appears once the film has shown them.
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): a hostile political quote is cut in as a broadcast clip and the chyron carries the attribution; the narrator never says who said it.

## The numbers

Ken Burns: attribution after every reading in 25 minutes read closely; readings begin from near silence (0.002) and are among the quietest cues. How Money Works: 3 quotes as clips in one montage (1:59 to 2:34).

## The effect

The quote is heard on its own terms before the listener knows whose it is, so the words are judged before the speaker; the name afterwards is the proof, like a footnote.

## Counter-example

Fireship names the executive before the quote ("a named executive answers the only way Nvidia knows how"), because the slight is the point and needs the name first. A quote in a learner video whose source is unknown is unverified material, which the lane's rules route to a concept scene.

## What it needs

A quote scene kind: the words on the frame or in a second voice, the attribution entering on a beat after the last word, the evidence id beside it.
