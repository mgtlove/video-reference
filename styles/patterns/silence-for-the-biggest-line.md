# Silence before the biggest line

Group: voice-and-sound. Say it like: "pause before the punchline", "let it breathe", "write in silence", "a beat before the answer".

## Observed

- Casually Explained (tqcThEqoYmA) 4:58: the only real pause in the video, 1.7 s, between "when is it okay to violate your moral principles?" and "Exactly. It has to be at least six figures."
- IBM Technology (bs1qPy_CWkM): six silences of 2 to 5 s (4.4 s at 4:29, 3.8 s at 5:52, 3.7 s at 2:33) where the longer words are written on the board; the edit keeps them.
- Ken Burns and PBS (g5WkOr565I4): 34 caption gaps over 2 s in the episode, 136 s in total; a 68 s hold with no words under the titles; readings begin from near silence ("To fight with whom?" at 0.002).

## The numbers

Casually: 1 pause in 371 s, placed on the one line the essay exists for. IBM: 74 pauses of 0.3 s or more, 62 s of silence in 7 minutes, 28 percent of samples under 0.02. Ken Burns: 1.9 pauses a minute in the voice.

## The effect

A silence is the loudest thing a level voice can do: it marks the next line as the one to hear. In the lightboard the silence is also where the eye watches the word appear, so nothing is lost; in the documentary it is where the viewer does the work.

## Counter-example

Fireship and How Money Works never pause, so their biggest lines are marked by the cut or the picture instead. A pause with nothing happening on the frame is dead air; the three that use it all give the eye something (a held image, a word drawing, a landscape).

## What it needs

A beat with no narration and one thing happening on the frame; the clip's silence measured, not estimated (the narration tool reads the gap).
