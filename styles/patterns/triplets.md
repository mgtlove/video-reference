# Triplets

Group: argument. Say it like: "three things", "rule of three", "list it in threes", "planning, execution, verification".

## Observed

- IBM Technology (bs1qPy_CWkM): 11 triplets in 64 sentences ("understand intent, assess impact, and reason"; "execution, validation, dependencies, and testing"; "planning to architecture to governance"), each written on the board as three items.
- Ken Burns and PBS (g5WkOr565I4): "lived and farmed and hunted"; "laws, levied taxes, and decided how they'd be spent"; "politically, economically, and militarily."
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): the stakes in a triple at 0:00 ("global trade, global finance, and the global balance of power").

## The numbers

IBM: one triplet every six sentences. Casually's lists escalate to a stupid last item instead (4 of them).

## The effect

Three is the smallest number that reads as a pattern and the largest that is held in one breath; a triplet on a board is also a complete picture. The comedians break the rule on purpose with a fourth item that does not belong.

## Counter-example

AWS lists six outline items and six questions, which is why they are written on screen as a checklist; a six-item list in the voice alone would not be held.

## What it needs

Nothing in the rig; a narration rule and a three-item card scene.
