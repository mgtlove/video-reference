# Only structural text on the frame

Group: word-and-picture. Say it like: "no text on screen", "keep the words in the voice", "just the chapter card", "numbers spoken not written".

## Observed

- AWS Developers (FAgmR9VV0GQ): the only text the channel adds is structural: the presenter's name, the question tabs, the checklist, the chapter cards, the end card. No number is written except inside the product.
- Ken Burns and PBS (g5WkOr565I4): no text on the frame other than titles and interviewees' names; every number and every name is spoken.
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): broadcast clips carry all the on-screen text; the channel adds none except a checklist and a map label; numbers are spoken.

## The numbers

AWS: 107 shots, text in the chapter cards and checklist only. Ken Burns: 0 numbers written in 114 minutes. How Money Works: 1 checklist, 1 map label, 1 table in 195 shots.

## The effect

With nothing to read, the viewer listens, and the voice can carry precision (a date, an IP address, a percentage) at speaking pace. Structural text (a chapter, a checklist) tells the viewer where they are without competing with what is being said.

## Counter-example

Fireship writes numbers (a chart, percentages) and caps titles per idea; Maarek writes everything the voice says; Casually hand-letters labels and prices in red. Text on the frame is the comedians' and the lecturer's instrument and the narrator's and the walkthrough's absence.

## What it needs

A restraint rule in `COPY` (kickers, chapter cards, a checklist; no numbers, no sentences), and captions as the one text layer when they are needed.
