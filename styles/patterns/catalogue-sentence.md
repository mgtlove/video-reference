# The catalogue sentence over landscape

Group: argument. Say it like: "list the places", "show the scope", "a slow list over wide shots", "everywhere at once".

## Observed

- Ken Burns and PBS (g5WkOr565I4) 4:32 to 7:30: a list of place names (rivers, old names then new names) read slowly over present-day landscape at low light; the device for "everywhere." The catalogue sentences are the slowest passages (99 wpm in the open) and the ones most likely to be remembered as scope.
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): a flag per country at 0.5 to 1 s per flag while the voice lists them.

## The numbers

Ken Burns: landscapes 31 percent of shots, held 3 to 5 s each inside the catalogue; the catalogue is the slowest speech in the film. How Money Works: the fastest cuts in the video are the list.

## The effect

A list read slowly over wide, calm pictures makes the viewer feel extent rather than count it; the same list cut fast over flags makes it a tally. Both say "many"; the pace says what kind of many.

## Counter-example

IBM writes the list on the board one item at a time, which makes it a structure, not a scope; Maarek reads a list of six routing policies in one run and shows nothing.

## What it needs

A sequence of stills or landscapes with a drift on each (`drift-on-every-still`), a list read at the narrator's slowest pace, no text.
