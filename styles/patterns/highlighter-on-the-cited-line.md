# The highlighter on the cited line

Group: word-and-picture. Say it like: "show the quote in the document", "highlight the line as it is read", "evidence on screen", "the source, not a slide about the source".

## Observed

- Fireship (I_KVMFrUtPk) 0:00 to 0:19: the phrase "wouldn't take no for an answer" is a quotation from the evidence document, highlighted on screen at the moment it is said; evidence is shown whole, then pushed in to the highlighted line.
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI) 3:00, 3:13, 3:29, 7:07: when the voice cites Article 49 or the law professor, a printed page lies on the desk and a highlighter moves along the line being read. These are the only moments in the video where the frame reads with the voice rather than under it.

## The numbers

Fireship: 24 evidence shots, 25 percent of running time, the licence page among the six longest holds (11.5 to 23.5 s). How Money Works: 4 highlighter moments in 195 shots.

## The effect

Reading with the voice turns a claim into a citation: the viewer sees the words on the page as they are spoken and does not have to take the narrator's word. Pushing in from the whole document to the line keeps the context (what the document is) and the proof (the line) in one move.

## Counter-example

Maarek and IBM cite nothing and show no document; their authority is the structure. Ken Burns shows the artefact but reads it in an actor's voice with the attribution after, so the picture is the object, not the text.

## What it needs

An evidence still with an id, a highlight that draws on at the moment the words are said, and a push-in (`camEl`, `camTravel`) from the whole to the line.
