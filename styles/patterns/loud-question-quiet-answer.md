# Loud question, quiet answer

Group: voice-and-sound. Say it like: "drop the voice for the conclusion", "understate the verdict", "emphasis by getting quieter", "raise the question".

## Observed

- IBM Technology (bs1qPy_CWkM): the loudest lines are the questions ("How do we know it worked?" "What changed?") and the thesis restatement; the three quietest lines are the three conclusions ("That's output." 0.062; "The future is prompt to production." 0.078; "It should generate evidence." 0.087).
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): the narrator drops for the understated verdicts ("a pretty sensible little arrangement," 0.082; "it's not great," 0.092) and is loudest in the argument (7:00 to 8:10).
- Casually Explained (tqcThEqoYmA): set-ups slightly louder, punchlines quieter; the two biggest jokes are delivered under the median loudness.

## The numbers

IBM: the loudest cues (the thesis restatement at 0.185, the questions beside it in the top list) against conclusions at 0.062 to 0.087, roughly 2 to 3 times louder. Casually: loudest set-up 0.109 against the moral question at 0.041.

## The effect

A dropped voice makes the listener lean in, and a conclusion delivered quietly sounds already settled; a raised question sounds open. The shape is measurable and the opposite of the natural habit of stressing the conclusion.

## Counter-example

Fireship's peaks sit on the promise and the verdict (0.214 at "promising, but it's not perfect"), structural emphasis rather than dropped; Maarek's loudest line is the voiced reply inside the story, and his conclusion is among the quietest for the other reason (the bell is near). AWS is loudest on instructions.

## What it needs

A narration direction note per sentence (question up, conclusion down) that survives into a generated voice, or a human read; nothing in the rig.
