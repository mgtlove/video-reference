# The cut voice

Group: voice-and-sound. Say it like: "tight edit", "cut the breaths", "fast but not rushed", "no dead air", "how fast can we talk".

## Observed

- Fireship (I_KVMFrUtPk): no pause of 0.3 s or more anywhere in five minutes at a low threshold; the breaths are cut; 220 wpm.
- Casually Explained (tqcThEqoYmA): seven pauses of 0.3 s or more in six minutes, one over a second, kept for the biggest line; 216 wpm.
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): one pause of 0.3 s in seventeen minutes, under a continuous music bed; 198 wpm.

## The numbers

Dynamic range 17.5, 18.2 and 16.4 dB (99th over 10th percentile of loudness); spectral centroid ranges narrow (Fireship 1165 to 1883 Hz); words at a normal syllable rate with the gaps removed, which is what makes 200 to 220 wpm sound unhurried.

## The effect

The words are not faster, the silence is gone; the voice is compressed and level so nothing is loud or quiet, and emphasis has to come from the cut, the picture or the word choice. The listener never waits.

## Counter-example

The teaching voices (IBM 63.5 dB, Maarek 32.4 dB, AWS 30.8 dB) keep their pauses because the pauses are real: a word being written, a model being waited for, a breath at a slide change. The narrator (Ken Burns) uses silence as structure. A cut voice over a frame that needs time to read leaves the viewer behind.

## What it needs

A voice route that returns tight clips (or an edit pass that removes gaps), and a part timing that measures the exact clip (kit v0.2.1).
