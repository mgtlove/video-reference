# A drawn figure over a real photograph

Group: word-and-picture. Say it like: "stick figure on the screenshot", "cartoon reaction on a real image", "the photo is the straight man".

## Observed

- Casually Explained (tqcThEqoYmA): real photographs are the straight man and the joke is in what is drawn or written on them: a tear, a label, a speech bubble, the host's face on a stranger, a price in red. A real news headline is read verbatim and the engineer is drawn beside it "pointing at the screen."
- Fireship (I_KVMFrUtPk): a fake caption on a real photo (the whisper caption, the sofa caption) and a label on a real image (the twins).

## The numbers

Casually: 31 shots with the drawn host, 25 of 78 frames carrying a visual joke, most of them a drawing or a label on a photograph. One black line weight, one red accent, white ground.

## The effect

The photograph supplies reality and the drawing supplies the attitude; neither has to be good at the other's job. A drawn figure gives a real screen a human reaction without a presenter.

## Counter-example

AWS, Maarek and IBM never draw on a screen; Ken Burns never draws at all. The drawing on a photo is the comedians' device, and the moment the drawing is on a product screen the screen stops being evidence.

## What it needs

A character layer that can sit beside, not on, a recreated screen (the template's rule), hand lettering as a face token, one accent.
