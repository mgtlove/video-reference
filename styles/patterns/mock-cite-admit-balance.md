# Mock, cite, admit, balance

Group: argument. Say it like: "make fun of it then explain it", "joke before the fact", "the definition section", "sceptical explainer".

## Observed

- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI) 2:39 to 3:50: the term is mocked first ("the kind of title you give to the CEO's useless child"), then the law is cited (Article 49), then a geographic joke, then the admission that no definition exists, then "all rules are made up," then a named academic for balance, then a second joke-definition ("situationship").
- Fireship (I_KVMFrUtPk) 0:30 to 0:55: the establishment's answer explained fairly in two sentences first, then undercut, so the mockery reads as earned.

## The numbers

How Money Works: 1.2 jokes a minute, almost all asides inside a factual sentence; the definitions section is the joke-densest minute. Fireship: fair explanation first, then the joke, in 25 s.

## The effect

The joke lowers the guard and the citation raises the floor; the admission and the balancing source make the narrator look fair while the jokes make him look unimpressed. The order matters: mockery after a fair statement is earned, before it is cheap.

## Counter-example

The teachers never mock; Casually never cites. In a learner video the mockery lands on the product being taught, so the pattern survives only as its middle (cite, admit, balance).

## What it needs

A narration shape per definition (cite, admit the gap, balance), and an evidence still for the citation.
