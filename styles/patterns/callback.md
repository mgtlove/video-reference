# The callback

Group: argument. Say it like: "bring it back", "tie it to the earlier point", "the same idea twice", "close the loop".

## Observed

- Fireship (I_KVMFrUtPk): "don't take no for an answer" returns at 0:28; "the same idea Nvidia had, they just did it with a processor and Archestra did it with a TOML file" at 3:26 ties the sponsor's answer to the establishment's, which is the intellectual spine of the video.
- IBM Technology (bs1qPy_CWkM): "idea to outcome" at 0:25 and 7:05; the slogan at 0:00, 6:44, 6:47; "execution" and "verification" in every movement.
- Casually Explained (tqcThEqoYmA): the confession at the open (dropped out) returns as the sponsor pivot ("I'm deeply scared of things that physically exist").

## The numbers

Fireship: 2 callbacks in 5 minutes. IBM: the slogan 3 times, the frame phrase twice, the naive view overturned 3 times.

## The effect

A callback makes two points one point; the viewer who followed both feels the structure close. In Fireship it is the argument (both answers are the same answer); in IBM it is the memory aid.

## Counter-example

AWS and Maarek do not call back; they restate (the close lists the stack; the two-step summary). Ken Burns' narrator repeats nothing; the thesis is restated by different voices instead.

## What it needs

A storyboard note linking the callback beat to its origin beat, and an element that can return (the same card, the same stroke) so the picture calls back too.
