# The camera is the reading

Group: word-and-picture. Say it like: "ken burns it", "move over the still", "pan down the list as I read it", "slow push on the document".

## Observed

- Ken Burns and PBS (g5WkOr565I4): the move over a still is the act of reading it. The "Join, or Die" headline is revealed by the move on the last word of the sentence; the camera travels down a list as the voice reads the items; a route draws on a period map as the sentence runs. Landscapes carry the catalogue sentences and the readings.

## The numbers

Drift or push on 82 of 118 logged rows; about 3 percent of frame height per second; 5 to 8 percent scale change over a hold; mean shot about 17 s, portraits held to 34 s, a dark press held 43 s; 123 wpm (99 in the open).

## The effect

A still becomes a sentence-length event: the eye travels with the voice and arrives with it, so a 20 s hold on one object has a beginning, a middle and an end. The reveal on the last word makes the picture the proof of the sentence.

## Counter-example

Maarek and AWS never move the camera; Fireship uses quick scale-ins only; IBM uses three fixed framings. A move on a still that is not timed to the sentence is wallpaper (How Money Works' drift, which is there to stop a still reading as a slide, not to read it).

## What it needs

`camTravel` and `camEl` with ease over the sentence's length, the reveal point placed on the last word, and nothing else on the frame while the camera moves.
