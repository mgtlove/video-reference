# Three registers of voice

Group: voice-and-sound. Say it like: "narrator and quotes", "let someone else say it", "the witness says what the narrator cannot", "read the document in a different voice".

## Observed

- Ken Burns and PBS (g5WkOr565I4): a narrator (past tense, declarative, no first person, no hedging, no judgement), document voices (actors reading period texts, present tense, attribution after), and witnesses (historians, present tense, first person plural, hesitations kept, modern idiom and humour allowed). Rotated on a cycle of 30 to 90 s; a witness and a document voice answer each other (the historian says Washington thought the British foolish, then Washington's letter says it in his own register).

## The numbers

In 25 minutes: the narrator's single rhetorical question is inside a quoted Native voice; attribution after every reading; hedges only where the record is uncertain; witnesses 10 percent of shots, artefacts 46 percent, landscapes 31 percent.

## The effect

The myth and its complication can both be stated without the narrator contradicting himself: the narrator carries facts, the documents carry proof, the witnesses carry judgement. The viewer is never told what to feel by the voice that told them what happened.

## Counter-example

Every other video studied has one voice that does all three jobs. How Money Works assigns the hostile quotes to broadcast clips, which is the same move in a cheaper form (the chyron carries the attribution).

## What it needs

Two or three voices in the clips (a narrator, a quoted document, a quoted person), each with its own frame treatment, and attribution as a beat after the quote, not before.
