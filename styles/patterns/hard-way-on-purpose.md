# The hard way first, on purpose

Group: argument. Say it like: "show the raw API first", "understand before the shortcut", "why we are doing it this way", "justify the structure".

## Observed

- AWS Developers (FAgmR9VV0GQ): "Showing you how to use the converse API like this is essentially doing it the hard way and we're doing this on purpose" (13:32); "I promise that later in the video I'll show you a super simple way" (15:46); at the close, the reveal that the framework was calling the APIs she taught, "This should all seem a lot less magical now." The structure of the video is argued for inside the video.

## The numbers

Said three times in 44 minutes, at the point of most friction and at the end; "on purpose" 2, "under the hood" 3.

## The effect

Naming the cost of the structure while the viewer is paying it turns impatience into trust: the viewer knows the shortcut exists and is being withheld for a reason that will be shown. The payoff at the end makes the long middle retroactively necessary.

## Counter-example

Fireship shows the shortcut (a config file) and the hard way (a processor) as the same idea, in one callback, in 25 s. Maarek defers the hard parts to later lectures and says so. The move needs a long video; in a short one the justification is the whole runtime.

## What it needs

A spoken line at the point of friction and a payoff beat at the close; a storyboard that marks which scene pays which promise.
