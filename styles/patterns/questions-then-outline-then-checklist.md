# Questions, outline, checklist

Group: argument. Say it like: "open with the questions", "tell them what they will learn", "tick off the list", "promise then deliver".

## Observed

- AWS Developers (FAgmR9VV0GQ): six questions in the first 50 s, each a thing a beginner fears; the product in three sentences; the outline as a promise ("we'll start simple and add more advanced features as we go"), six verb phrases, two acronyms glossed; the question tabs on screen become the checklist the video ticks off; the close restates the stack one sentence per component.
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): three concrete questions a viewer would ask, the promise ("we will get to that"), and "four things to consider" announced at the turn.
- Fireship (I_KVMFrUtPk): a three-part teaser (what they did, the historical hook, a test with stakes) before the ident at 79 s.

## The numbers

AWS: 9 questions in 50 s, then almost none; the outline list of 6; a 44-minute video closed by restating the 6. Fireship: the ident at 1:19, after the hook has done its work.

## The effect

The viewer knows the shape of the video before it starts and can see progress through it; a question asked in the viewer's words is a promise that the video will answer it, and the checklist is the receipt. Restating the outline at the close is the summary for free.

## Counter-example

IBM opens with a contrast, not questions, and never outlines; Maarek has no promise beyond "we'll see this later"; Ken Burns opens with a reading and an explosion. A checklist for a 3-minute video is scaffolding heavier than the building.

## What it needs

A chapter-card scene kind, a checklist element that ticks on a beat, and a brief whose scene list is the checklist.
