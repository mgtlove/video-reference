# The reset frame

Group: word-and-picture. Say it like: "section break", "come back to the host", "chapter card", "wipe between sections", "a new board".

## Observed

- Casually Explained (tqcThEqoYmA): sections of 40 to 60 s are separated by the host alone on white.
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): "Okay, so" in the voice and the channel's green wipe and badge in the frame (5:17, 6:46), or a clipping opener posing the section's question.
- AWS Developers (FAgmR9VV0GQ): chapter cards and a blue flash; the question tabs at the open become the checklist that ticks off.
- IBM Technology (bs1qPy_CWkM): a new board for each of the four movements; the board change at 2:33 is a 3.7 s silence.
- Fireship (I_KVMFrUtPk): no spoken section markers; the ident at 1:19 is the one fixed break.

## The numbers

Casually: 10 sections in 6 minutes. How Money Works: 11 sections in 17 minutes, each opened by "Okay, so" or a question. IBM: 4 boards in 7 minutes. AWS: 6 chapters in 44 minutes.

## The effect

A reset tells the viewer the previous point is closed and resets attention; the same device every time becomes the channel's signature and lets the viewer predict the structure. A reset that is also a question (How Money Works' clipping) sets up the next section's job.

## Counter-example

Maarek has no resets beyond a new slide, and Ken Burns uses music and silence instead of a frame, so the structure is felt, not shown.

## What it needs

A scene kind for the break (a card, a wipe, a plain frame) with one token for its look, and a storyboard rule that each section opens with it.
