# The fixed lines

Group: argument. Say it like: "sign-off", "the same closing line every time", "house format", "the ident".

## Observed

- Fireship (I_KVMFrUtPk): the date stamp and ident at 1:19, after the hook; two sign-off lines; the sign-off is the quietest cue in the video (0.020).
- How Money Works (W6xkZy9nkaI): "learn how money works" sign-off; "Okay, so" as the section opener.
- AWS Developers (FAgmR9VV0GQ): "I'll see you in the next one," the loudest cue in the sampled video (0.117).
- Stephane Maarek (10JKpg-eqZU): "Happy learning" card; "okay so let's talk about" as the opener.
- IBM Technology (bs1qPy_CWkM): the thesis line at open and close ("prompt to production," 0:00, 6:44, 6:47).
- Casually Explained, Ken Burns: none.

## The numbers

Fireship: ident at 79 s in every episode; Fireship's sign-off at 0.020 and AWS's at 0.117, the two extremes of the set.

## The effect

A fixed line at a fixed place is the format's signature: the viewer knows the video has started properly (the ident after the hook) and has ended (the line). It also carries the channel name without a logo.

## Counter-example

The two with no fixed lines (Casually, Ken Burns) are the two with the strongest voice, which carries identity on its own. A fixed line that arrives before the hook (an ident at 0:00) delays the reason to watch.

## What it needs

`COPY` slots for an opener and a sign-off, and a brief rule for where the ident sits (after the hook, never first).
