# The reversal definition

Group: argument. Say it like: "define it as the opposite", "a one-line definition that is a joke", "taxonomy of types", "eleven kinds of".

## Observed

- Casually Explained (tqcThEqoYmA) 2:51 to 3:50: eleven one-line definitions, 1.5 to 6.5 s each, each a reversal (civil engineers "make targets," chemists are pharmacy cashiers, software engineers qualify for food stamps), one an empty slot ("Mechatronics. Oh, don't know how that got in there"). The densest minute: 11 definitions, 11 frames, each frame changing on the punchline word.

## The numbers

14 reversal definitions in 6 minutes; the taxonomy at 11 cuts in 59 s; the empty slot delivered under the median loudness.

## The effect

A taxonomy told as reversals teaches the real categories (the viewer has to know what a civil engineer does to get the joke) while never sounding like a list. The empty slot is the list admitting it is a list.

## Counter-example

Maarek's four DNS records are the straight form of the same move ("X which is URL to Y"), 1 definition per sentence, no reversal; AWS's example-list ("A, that's X. B, also X.") is the straight form with rhythm.

## What it needs

A card-per-item scene with a cut per item on the last word; the straight form needs only the cards.
