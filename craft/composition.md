## What a video maker needs to know

**Safe areas.** Broadcast standards assume the edge of the picture may be cut off or covered. SMPTE ST 2046-1 defines a safe action area of 93% of the width and 93% of the height, centred, and a safe title area of 90% by 90% (NAB, quoting the standard). EBU R95 says the same thing as margins: action safe is 3.5% in from every edge, graphics safe is 5% in from every edge, and "all graphics are framed in the Graphics Safe Area". Older editing software still defaults to the 1961 guides of 90% action and 80% title; Ward argues the 20% title crop "is unnecessary for modern editing". For online players nothing is cropped, but player controls, progress bars and overlays sit on the picture, and the standards' 5% margin remains the common modern practice because it is the one number everyone can agree on. Keep every word inside the 5% margin; keep everything that matters inside 3.5%.

**Rule of thirds.** Divide the frame with two vertical and two horizontal lines. A subject on a line or an intersection reads as composed; a centred subject reads as static or formal. Tomkies: "placing the subject off-center creates visual interest". Break it on purpose: a close-up of one control can sit dead centre, and that reads as emphasis.

**Reading order.** Western readers enter at the top left. On text-heavy layouts with no strong cues the eye traces an F (Pernice: "the F-pattern is the default pattern when there are no strong cues"). On sparse layouts with a few elements it traces a Z, top left to top right, diagonally to bottom left, then bottom right (Babich). The lesson is not to design for the F; it is to give strong cues so the eye goes where the voice is, and to put the first thing first in the top left.

**Negative space.** Empty space is not waste. The Edinburgh HCI notes: "sufficient whitespace makes a design easier to navigate". Space around a thing is what makes it a focus point; a frame with no empty space has no focus point.

**How much at once.** Cowan's review puts working memory at "about four chunks". Mayer's coherence principle says extraneous material is excluded; his signaling principle says main ideas are highlighted; spatial contiguity says words sit near the graphic they describe. Brame's summary of the same research: signal with "key words on screen highlighting important elements", and weed out music and complex backgrounds. Four things the viewer must hold at once is the ceiling, not the target.

**Alignment and grids.** An invisible grid makes a layout scannable; proximity says things that belong together sit together (Edinburgh). Misalignment of a few pixels reads as sloppiness before it reads as anything else.

**Highlights, pointers, callouts.** A callout must not cover what it points at. New Relic's rule for annotated screenshots: a two-pixel clearance between the element and the box, boxes "should not obstruct any of the elements they're calling out", and a text overlay must not overlap essential UI text. The same guide says a screenshot "shouldn't require a text callout 99 percent of the time": if the frame needs a label to be understood, the frame is wrong.

**Contrast.** WCAG 1.4.3: text at least 4.5:1 against its background, or 3:1 for large text (18 point, or 14 point bold). WCAG 1.4.11: interface parts and graphics needed to understand the content at least 3:1 against adjacent colours; ratios are not rounded, so 2.999:1 fails. These apply to our drawn text and callouts directly. The exception for text inside a picture with other content covers recreated screens, but a highlight box or a label we draw on top of one is ours to get right.

**Captions.** Netflix requires two lines at most, 42 characters per line, up to 20 characters per second for adults, each event on screen at least five-sixths of a second and at most seven seconds, and the subtitle "placed where easier to read" when it would collide with on-screen text. The BBC guidance, as summarised by Clevercast, gives 160 to 180 words per minute and two lines. Whatever the exact house numbers, the rule everyone shares is that captions never sit on top of the thing being taught.

**Colour balance.** The 60-30-10 rule: a dominant colour on about 60% of the frame, a secondary on 30%, an accent on 10% for focus points (Bistricer). It is a habit, not a standard, and it is the reason a highlight colour stops working when it is used on more than a tenth of the frame.

## For our stage

The stage is 1920x1080. On it, ST 2046-1 and R95 give the same pixels: action safe is 67 px in from the sides and 38 px from top and bottom (a 1786x1004 rectangle); title or graphics safe is 96 px from the sides and 54 px from top and bottom (a 1728x972 rectangle). Our own reserved spaces sit inside those: the right rail is x 1440 to 1920, the caption band is y 880 to 1080, so the picture area is 1440x880 at the top left. Text in the rail keeps 96 px from the right edge and 54 px from the top; text in the picture area keeps 96 px from the left edge and 54 px from the top, and stays out of the caption band entirely. The caption band is reserved space at all times, not only when a caption is showing, so that a caption can appear without anything moving.

The thirds that matter are the picture area's, not the whole frame's: vertical lines at x 480 and x 960, horizontal at y 293 and y 587. The upper-left intersection (480, 293) is where the eye enters; the upper-right (960, 293) sits on the vertical centre line of the whole frame and is the natural landing for a camera move. A focus point that lands in the lower band of thirds competes with the caption band. The rail's own thirds are narrow; the key phrase sits in the rail's upper two thirds, left-aligned to the rail's inner margin, so the reading order is picture, then rail, then caption.

Reading order on our stage is a Z that has been bent: the eye starts in the picture at top left, sweeps right into the rail for the key phrase, drops to the caption. The voice is the strongest cue and the camera follows it (see `camera.md`), so the F-pattern should never get a chance to appear; if a frame has enough text to invite an F, it has too much text.

Recreated screens are faithful to their screenshots, so the composition tool for them is the camera, not the layout: we do not move the buttons. A highlight sits on the control at its exact bounds plus a clearance; a pointer arrives at the control from the side the voice came from, never over its label; a callout sits beside the control with a gap, in the direction with the most empty space, and never over any other control. Concept scenes have free layout, so they get the grid: cards on a column grid, equal gutters, one accent colour.

What the rig already does right: the rail and the band are fixed regions, so nothing we compose can drift into them; `camTravel` lands the camera on a target we choose, so the thirds are a matter of choosing the target; `cursorTo` moves the pointer along a path, so its arrival side is ours to set. What it does not do: it does not know the safe margins, it does not check that a callout clears its target, and it does not count how much is on screen. Those are checks on rendered frames and reviewer judgement.

## Rules

| Id | Rule | Why | Checkable |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-COMP-1 | Every glyph of drawn text (rail, callout, label, caption) lies inside the title-safe rectangle: 96 px from the sides, 54 px from top and bottom. | SMPTE ST 2046-1 90% title safe; EBU R95 5% graphics safe. | yes: bounding box of rendered text, no edge closer than 96 px (x) or 54 px (y) to the frame edge |
| C-COMP-2 | Everything the viewer must see (a highlighted control, a card, a character) lies inside the action-safe rectangle: 67 px from the sides, 38 px from top and bottom. | SMPTE ST 2046-1 93% action safe; EBU R95 3.5% action safe. | yes: bounding box of each subject, no edge closer than 67 px (x) or 38 px (y) |
| C-COMP-3 | No drawn element other than a caption enters the caption band (y 880 to 1080), whether or not a caption is showing. | Captions must not cover the thing being taught (Netflix, BBC as summarised); reserving the band means nothing moves when a caption appears. | yes: no non-caption pixel drawn in y >= 880 in the picture area or rail |
| C-COMP-4 | The subject of a shot lands on or within a set distance of one of the picture area's third intersections (480 or 960, 293 or 587), or dead centre of the picture area when the shot is a single control chosen for emphasis. | Rule of thirds (Tomkies); centring for emphasis is the deliberate exception. | yes: subject centre within 80 px of an intersection or of (720, 440); the 80 px tolerance is a house choice, not a sourced number |
| C-COMP-5 | Prefer the upper two intersections; a subject in the lower band is allowed only when no caption is due during the shot. | The eye enters top left; the lower band competes with the caption band. | judgement |
| C-COMP-6 | At most four things ask for attention in a frame: subject, highlight, rail phrase, caption count as separate things; a card grid counts as one thing when its cards are alike. | Cowan, about four chunks; Mayer coherence. | yes: count of distinct attention-bearing elements <= 4; what counts as one element is judgement |
| C-COMP-7 | A callout, label or highlight never covers any pixel of the control it refers to, nor any other control; it keeps a clearance from its target. | New Relic annotation rule; Mayer spatial contiguity puts it near, not on. | yes: callout box and target box do not intersect; gap >= 2 px (New Relic's number); house default 8 px on our stage |
| C-COMP-8 | A callout sits on the side of its target with the most empty space, and its pointer or arrow touches the target's edge, not its interior. | Proximity and clear reference; the viewer should not search for what a label means. | judgement, with a measurable part: arrow tip within 8 px of the target's bounding box edge and outside it |
| C-COMP-9 | Text we draw has at least 4.5:1 contrast against what is behind it, or 3:1 when 18 pt or larger, or 14 pt bold or larger, at stage scale. | WCAG 1.4.3. | yes: sampled contrast ratio of text colour against its background at rendered size; thresholds 4.5:1 and 3:1, not rounded |
| C-COMP-10 | Highlight boxes, arrows, pointers and any drawn graphic needed to follow the lesson have at least 3:1 contrast against the pixels adjacent to them, including against a recreated screen. | WCAG 1.4.11 non-text contrast. | yes: contrast of the stroke colour against the sampled adjacent background >= 3:1 |
| C-COMP-11 | Elements in a concept scene sit on a shared column grid with equal gutters; edges that should align do align. | Grids and proximity (Edinburgh HCI notes). | yes: edges meant to align differ by <= 2 px; gutters within 2 px of each other |
| C-COMP-12 | A frame keeps empty space around the subject; the subject's bounding box does not touch another element's box. | Negative space makes the focus point (Edinburgh). | yes: gap between the subject box and any other element box >= 24 px; the 24 px is a house choice |
| C-COMP-13 | The accent colour (highlight, pointer, key phrase emphasis) covers no more than about a tenth of the frame. | 60-30-10 (Bistricer); an accent used everywhere marks nothing. | yes: accent-colour pixel share <= 10% of the frame |
| C-COMP-14 | A caption is at most two lines and 42 characters per line, and stays on screen at least five-sixths of a second and at most seven seconds. | Netflix timed text requirements; BBC as summarised agrees on two lines. | yes: line count, characters per line, on-screen duration from the timeline |
| C-COMP-15 | Rail text is left-aligned to the rail's inner margin and sits in the rail's upper two thirds. | Reading order picture, rail, caption; the rail is narrow and its lower third meets the caption band. | yes: rail text box top >= 54 px, bottom <= 640 px, left edge at the rail's inner margin |
| C-COMP-16 | Recreated screens keep their real layout; composition on them is done with the camera, highlight and pointer, never by moving controls. | Fidelity to the screenshot is a product requirement; a viewer who then opens the product must find things where the video showed them. | yes: rendered control positions match the screenshot within scale; see `camera.md` for the scale rule |

## Turned down

Formal tone keeps every rule above; safe areas, contrast and the reserved caption band are not stylistic. What it changes: the accent share drops well under 10% (one highlight at a time, no pointer trail), cards sit on a plainer grid with more empty space, and the rail carries fewer words. It must not change: text sizes below the contrast thresholds, the caption band's size, or the callout clearance. Turning motion down (see `camera.md`) makes composition carry more of the work, so in formal tone the thirds and the negative space are checked more strictly, not less.
